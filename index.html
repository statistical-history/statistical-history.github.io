01 / The course

Statistics is more than a toolbox.

Its history is a history of reasoning with imperfect information.

The seminar approaches statistics as an intellectual history and a living practice: a collection of ideas that reshaped how people describe uncertainty, make comparisons, and argue from evidence.

I

History

How counting people, measuring variation, and governing by numbers changed what societies could see.

II

Ideas

How probability, models, uncertainty, and causation gave evidence a formal language.

III

Evidence

How statistical arguments gain authority—and where their assumptions, omissions, and values enter.

02 / Guiding questions

The questions behind the calculations.

The seminar asks how technical ideas connect to the human problems that give them meaning.

  1. 01

    What becomes visible when the world is translated into numbers?

  2. 02

    What makes one piece of evidence more persuasive than another?

  3. 03

    How should we judge a statistical claim—and what might it leave out?
